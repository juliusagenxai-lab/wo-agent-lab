The last thing I expected to find behind the gymnasium was a door. Not a janitor's closet or a bathroom — a full-sized wooden door, painted midnight blue, standing perfectly upright in the middle of the grass with no wall attached to it. Its brass handle was shaped like a fox.

I looked around. The other kids were still at lunch. I grabbed the handle.

The door swung open to reveal a world soaked in amber light. Towering trees with silver bark lined a cobblestone path that wound up toward a city floating in the clouds. Strange birds — small, with wings like stained glass — circled overhead, casting rainbow shadows on the ground. The air smelled like rain and cinnamon.

My heart was hammering, but my feet had already decided. I stepped through.

A girl my age appeared from behind one of the silver trees. She wore a coat covered in small glowing buttons, and she was looking at me like I was the strange thing in this world.

"You're late," she said, handing me a folded piece of paper. "The Council has been waiting."

I unfolded it. At the top, in handwriting I somehow recognized as my own, were three words: Save the door.

I didn't know what it meant yet. But I knew one thing — I wasn't going back to lunch.
